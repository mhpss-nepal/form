Situation Overview
On 26 August 2026, a major ice–rock avalanche occurred in the upper Bhote Koshi watershed near the China–
Nepal border, approximately 20 km upstream of Rasuwagadhi. Preliminary analysis based on high-resolution 
satellite imagery indicates that the event originated from a north-facing slope near the Lhende River, where an 
estimated 1 km² section of rock-glacier material detached and rapidly moved downslope. The detached ice and 
rock mass generated an ice–rock avalanche that entrained loose proglacial sediments along its downstream 
path, transforming into a highly mobile sediment- and water-laden debris flow. Initial assessments suggest that 
the resulting outburst flood in the upper watershed exceeded the magnitude of normal seasonal flooding by an 
order of magnitude or more.
A seismic signal equivalent to a magnitude greater than 5 Mw earthquake was recorded near the Langtang/
Lirung area, close to the identified avalanche source zone. Subsequent analysis confirmed that the seismic signal

river damming, lake breach floods, additional debris flows and secondary flooding events.
The event affected the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli–Narayani river corridor, a critical north–south transboundary 
river system extending from the Tibet–Nepal border through Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, Chitwan, 
T anahun and Nawalparasi. The corridor contains strategically important infrastructure, including the 
Rasuwagadhi border crossing, customs and immigration facilities, major settlements, tourism routes, public 
services, and hydropower projects such as Rasuwagadhi, Chilime and Trishuli. Rasuwa represents a particularly 
vulnerable section due to its steep terrain, active geological conditions, concentrated settlements, and critical 
infrastructure located within a narrow mountain valley. At approximately 08:40 hrs on 26 August 2026, an ice–
rock avalanche in the upper watershed near the Nepal–China border affected Lhende Khola, generating a large 
flood and debris flow that entered the Bhote Koshi River. The Bhote Koshi–Syafrubesi hydrological station 
stopped transmitting data at around 08:50 hrs, with the last recorded water level of 1.62 m. By 09:15 hrs, DHM 
confirmed a major flood wave and issued downstream warnings for communities along the Trishuli corridor.
The flood wave rapidly propagated downstream, reaching Galchhi (10:28 hrs), Malekhu (11:50 hrs), Mugling 
(13:00 hrs), and Devghat (15:20 hrs). At Devghat, the peak discharge reached approximately 5,850 m³ /s at 
16:00 hrs, with an estimated total flood volume of 57.40 million m³, including approximately 19.96 million m³ 
of excess flood water above base flow. A total of 679,295 warning SMS messages were disseminated through 
Nepal T elecom and Ncell to downstream riverine communities. The event transformed from a high-altitude 
transboundary ice–rock avalanche into a multi-hazard flood and debris-flow emergency, affecting multiple 
districts along the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli–Narayani corridor. Ongoing risks remain from temporary river 
blockages, sudden breach floods, additional debris flows and rainfall-triggered secondary hazards, requiring 
continued monitoring, early warning and coordinated disaster response.
Key Response Activities
• The verified list of persons rescued from various locations is available on the NDRRMA website:  
ndrrma.gov.np/np/rescue.   
• Indian, Chinese, Korean and Nepali  rescue teams continue to be mobilized for joint search and rescue 
operations at hydropower project sites, including Trishuli 3A, Haku Besi, Trishuli 3B, and Chilime.
• Command Post activated, and 15 Thematic Emergency Coordination T eams mobilized  to coordinate 
the response, relief delivery and restoration of health, communication, road, electricity and other critical 
infrastructure and services.
• Given the disruption to transport and communication links, priority attention given to northeast  Rasuwa, 
including Syafru, Timure and surrounding villages.
• For the safety and security of rescue flights , drones may be operated only with prior approval  from the 
relevant District Administration Office or the Ministry of Home Affairs (MoHA).
• Digital relief-management system introduced to improve coordination and prevent duplication. 
• Relief supplies  arriving by air are managed through Emergency Warehouse at Tribhuvan International 
Airport, while supplies arriving by road are being managed through collection centres in Samakhusi, Battar 
(Nuwakot) and Dhunche (Rasuwa).

Sector Reported impact
Housing & Shelter • Housing damage and exposure: In Nuwakot, 1,267 houses have been reported as fully 
damaged and 1,253 as partially damaged. Satellite mapping identified 4,689 buildings within 
the flood-affected zone along the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor. This figure represents 
estimated exposure, not verified damage.
• Holding centres: A total of 30 centres are accommodating 3,930 people across Nuwakot and 
Rasuwa.
• Nuwakot: 19 centers are accommodating 2,653 people, including 13 in Bidur Municipality, 
4 in Belkotgadhi Municipality and 2 in Kispang Rural Municipality.
• Rasuwa: Eleven centres are accommodating 1,277 people across Uttargaya, Kalika, 
Aamachhodingmo and Gosaikunda rural municipalities.
• Relief collection points: One collection point in Kathmandu and five in Nuwakot and Rasuwa 
are supporting the coordination of humanitarian relief supplies.
Roads & Bridges • A total of 41 bridges have been washed away and 4 bridges damaged. 
• Prithvi Highway (Mugling–Dhading section): Transportation is completely obstructed due to 
toe cutting at Krishna Bhir and Parewa Bhir.
• Rasuwa District:
• The Trishuli–Mailung–Syabrubesi road section at Khalte, Uttargaya Rural Municipality–5 
remains completely obstructed due to flooding, and the disruption is expected to continue 
for a prolonged period.
• All main highways in Rasuwa District are reported as completely obstructed until further 
notice due to flooding.
• Nuwakot District:
• On the T okha–Chhahare road section at Ghattekhola, Shivapuri Rural Municipality–8, 
vehicular movement is restricted at night from 22:00 to 05:00 until further notice due to 
the potential risk of flooding and landslides.
• The Pasang Lhamu road section at Simtar, Belkotgadhi Municipality–10 is completely 
obstructed until further notice due to flooding.
• B.P . Highway – Kavre: Due to potential flooding, all vehicular movement on the Khurkot–
Nepalthok–Katunjebesi route is suspended from 18:00 to 04:00 until further notice. From 
04:00 to 18:00, vehicle movement may be allowed depending on prevailing conditions.
• Kanti Lokpath – Lalitpur: The highway at Bhattedanda, Bagmati Rural Municipality–3 is 
completely obstructed due to a landslide.
Education • Preliminary reporting covers 19 schools in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading, with a combined 
IEMIS enrolment of 6,901 students. 
• 8 schools are reported fully damaged, 8 partially damaged and 3 physically safe. Damage 
includes buildings washed away, buried under debris or mud, inundated or affected by land 
subsidence. Detailed verification of school safety, functionality and learning-continuity 
requirements is ongoing.
Energy • Rasuwagadhi, Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi, Chilime, Langtang Khola Hydroelectric Project, Upper

ongoing.
